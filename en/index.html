What a felucca trip is

A felucca is the old sailing boat of the Nile: a wooden hull, one huge triangular sail, no engine. Between Aswan and Edfu it is still the slowest and quietest way to travel the river, and the only one where you sleep on the water rather than in a cabin.

You live on the deck, which is one big cushioned platform under an awning. By day you watch the banks go by: palm groves, fields of sugar cane, children waving, fishermen in rowing boats. In the evening Tarek moors at an island or a sandy bank, cooks dinner, and you sleep on deck under the stars.

There is no timetable to keep up with. The north wind usually blows against you, so the felucca zigzags from bank to bank and the current does part of the work. That is why the route is always written as "up to": the wind decides how far we get.

A day on board, roughly

Sunrise
Tea on deck while the river is still flat and pink.
Morning
Breakfast, then sailing. Stops at a village or a temple when we pass one.
Midday
Lunch in the shade of the awning. In hot months, a long break.
Afternoon
More sailing, a nap, the banks going by.
Sunset
Tarek looks for a quiet place to moor for the night.
Evening
Dinner, the stars, and early to bed.

Life on board

The questions everyone asks before booking a felucca are about the toilet, the food and the night. Here are straight answers.

Hot meals cooked on board

Tarek cooks three meals a day on board, all served hot: soups, stews, rice, lentils, grilled fish or chicken, eggs, bread and fruit to peel. Egyptian home cooking, light and wholesome, just right after a day in the sun.

Tell Tarek if you are vegetarian or avoid something: he plans the shopping around it.

A typical day of meals

  • Breakfast: eggs, beans, cheese, bread, tea or coffee
  • Lunch and dinner: soup, rice or pasta, vegetables cooked in sauce, grilled fish or chicken
  • Between meals: tea, and fruit you peel yourself

A private toilet on board

A real toilet on the boat, just for your group, for the whole trip. Ask Tarek on WhatsApp for a photo before you book: he will send one.

Mattresses, cushions, blankets, shade

The deck is covered with mattresses and cushions: you sit on them by day and sleep on them at night. Blankets are provided, and an awning keeps the sun off during the day. Bring a warm layer from December to February: nights on the water are cold.

What a felucca is not

  • There is no engine. When the wind drops, we drift with the current and make tea.
  • There is no cabin. You sleep on deck, with the rest of your group.
  • From May to September it is over 40 °C in the afternoon. We sail early and late and rest in the shade.
  • On the first morning the boat stops at the river police post for the permit. It can take until noon.

The route, from Aswan downstream

The Nile flows north, so from Aswan you sail downstream. Here are the places on the way. How far we get depends on the wind: if it does not get us to the last stop, the last stretch is done by road.

  1. Aswan and Elephantine

    Departure among the granite islands of the first cataract. You pass Elephantine, the botanical garden on Kitchener's Island, the Aga Khan mausoleum and the Tombs of the Nobles cut into the hill of Qubbet el-Hawa.

  2. Villages and islands

    North of Aswan the city disappears quickly. Small villages, palm trees on the banks, and the first night moored at an island or a sandy beach.

  3. Daraw

    A market town known for its camel market, held on certain days of the week. Tell Tarek your dates: he will tell you whether the trip can fall on a market day.

  4. Kom Ombo

    End of the 3 days, 2 nights trip

    A double temple built for Sobek, the crocodile god, and Haroeris, standing right on the riverbank. Next to it, a small museum of mummified crocodiles. Best visited late in the afternoon.

  5. Gebel el-Silsila

    End of the 4 days, 3 nights trip

    The river narrows between two sandstone cliffs. This is where the pharaohs quarried stone for their temples: carved chapels, stelae and the rock temple of Horemheb. Few visitors, often nobody.

  6. Edfu

    End of the 5 days, 4 nights trip

    The temple of Horus, one of the best-preserved temples in Egypt. Arriving by boat early in the morning, you see it before the coaches. From Edfu, Luxor is about two hours by road.

Questions before you book

How much does a felucca cruise cost?

The rate depends on the trip, the season and the number of people on board. It covers the whole private boat, the crew and three meals a day. Tarek sends his price on WhatsApp within minutes. Temple tickets, the road transfer at the end and tips are extra.

Ask Tarek for the price

When is the best time to sail?

From October to April. December and January have mild days and cold nights on the water, so bring a warm layer. In March and April the days get hot again but the evenings are lovely.

Is it too hot in summer?

From May to September it often goes over 40 °C in the afternoon. The trip is still possible: we sail early in the morning and late in the day and spend the hottest hours under the awning. If you are sensitive to heat, the 3-day trip is the better choice in summer.

Is there a toilet on board?

Yes, a private toilet on board, just for your group, for the whole trip. Ask Tarek on WhatsApp for a photo before you book.

What do we eat on board?

Three meals a day, cooked on board by Tarek and served hot: soups, stews, rice, lentils, grilled fish or chicken, eggs, and fruit to peel. Light, homely Egyptian cooking. Vegetarians are welcome: say so when you book.

Is a felucca trip safe?

Every departure is registered with the tourist police: Tarek needs a copy of your passport at least two days before to get the permit. The boat is private, so only your group is on board, and the two crew members are with you for the whole trip.

Where and how do we sleep?

On deck, on mattresses with cushions and blankets, under the sky. The boat is moored for the night at an island or a quiet bank. Bring a warm layer and, from November to February, a sleeping bag liner if you feel the cold.

What should we bring?

A small day bag (big bags are stored out of the way), a warm layer, a head torch, a hat, sunscreen, mosquito repellent, a towel, sandals, a power bank, and cash in Egyptian pounds for temple tickets and tips. And your passport.

Can we sail all the way to Luxor?

Not by felucca: Aswan to Luxor is too far for a sailing boat in a few days. The longest trip ends in Edfu, two hours from Luxor by road. Ask Tarek about the car when you book.

Someone on the Corniche says he is Tarek. Is he?

Touts on the Aswan Corniche sometimes pretend to be the captain a traveller is looking for. Tarek only books through his WhatsApp number on this site. If in doubt, write to that number before paying anyone.
