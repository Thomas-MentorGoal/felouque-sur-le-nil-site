What a felucca trip is

A felucca is the traditional sailing boat of the Nile: a wooden hull, one big triangular sail, no engine. The boat sails downstream from Aswan, and you sleep on deck, not in a cabin.

The deck is a platform covered with mattresses and cushions, under an awning. You spend the day and the night there. In the evening Tarek moors at an island or a sandy bank and serves dinner on board.

The north wind usually blows against you: the felucca tacks from bank to bank and the current carries it. So the distance covered each day depends on the wind.

Life on board

Hot meals cooked on board

Tarek cooks three meals a day on board, all served hot: soups, stews, rice, lentils, grilled fish or chicken, eggs, bread and fruit to peel. Egyptian home cooking.

Tell Tarek if you are vegetarian or avoid something: he plans the shopping around it.

A typical day of meals

  • Breakfast: eggs, beans, cheese, bread, tea or coffee
  • Lunch and dinner: soup, rice or pasta, vegetables cooked in sauce, grilled fish or chicken
  • Between meals: tea, and fruit you peel yourself

A private toilet on board

A toilet on the boat, for your group only, for the whole trip.

Sleeping on deck

The deck is covered with mattresses and cushions: you sit on them by day and sleep on them at night. Blankets are provided, and an awning keeps the sun off during the day. Bring a warm layer from December to February: nights on the water are cold.

What a felucca is not

  • There is no engine. When the wind drops, the boat moves with the current.
  • There is no cabin. You sleep on deck, with the rest of your group.
  • From May to September it is over 40 °C in the afternoon. We sail early and late and rest in the shade.

The route, from Aswan downstream

The Nile flows north: from Aswan, the felucca goes downstream, from island to island. Here is Tarek's programme, stop by stop.

  1. Aswan

    Departure around noon, among the granite islands of the First Cataract. Leaving the town northwards, the boat passes Elephantine and Kitchener's Island. An hour of sailing, then lunch on board.

  2. Daraw Island

    An afternoon of sailing, and you arrive at sunset. Dinner and the first night on board.

  3. Kom Ombo

    A double temple, dedicated to Sobek, the crocodile god, and to Haroeris, on the bank of the river. You reach it on the morning of day two, for a visit of an hour and a half.

  4. Basheer Island and Gebel el-Silsila

    Lunch around 1 pm on Basheer Island. Then sailing until sunset, and arrival at the temple of Gebel el-Silsila, where the river narrows between two sandstone cliffs: the pharaohs cut the stone of their temples here.

  5. Basaw Island

    End of the 3 days, 2 nights trip

    Second night on board, moored at Basaw Island: tea on an island, then dinner.

  6. Rudisia and Fawza islands

    On day three, a stop on Rudisia Island for lunch, then sailing to Fawza Island at sunset. Tea, dinner, an evening of songs and the last night on board.

  7. Edfu, temple of Horus and Luxor

    End of the 4 days, 3 nights trip

    A last morning of sailing to Edfu. A private minibus takes you to the temple of Horus, one of the best preserved in Egypt, then to Luxor.

This programme adapts to your constraints: length, departure date, arrival or train time, pace, stops. Tell Tarek on WhatsApp when you book.

Questions before you book

How much does a felucca cruise cost?

The price depends on the trip, the season and the number of people on board. It covers the whole private boat, the crew and the three meals of the day. Tarek sends you his price on WhatsApp within minutes. Temple tickets are extra.

Ask Tarek for the price

How far ahead should we book?

About a month ahead is best. You can also write to Tarek at the last minute: he replies and tells you if the boat is free.

When is the best time to sail?

From October to April. December and January have mild days and cold nights on the water, so bring a warm layer. In March and April the days get hot again.

Is it too hot in summer?

From May to September it often goes over 40 °C in the afternoon. The trip is still possible: we sail early in the morning and late in the day and spend the hottest hours under the awning. If you are sensitive to heat, the 3-day trip is the better choice in summer.

Is there a toilet on board?

Yes, a private toilet on board, just for your group, for the whole trip.

What do we eat on board?

Three meals a day, cooked on board by Tarek and served hot: soups, stews, rice, lentils, grilled fish or chicken, eggs, and fruit to peel. Egyptian home cooking. Vegetarians are welcome: say so when you book.

Is a felucca trip safe?

The boat is private: only your group is on board, and Captain Tarek and Samir are with you for the whole trip.

Where and how do we sleep?

On deck, on mattresses with cushions and blankets, under the sky. The boat is moored for the night at an island or a quiet bank. Bring a warm layer and, from November to February, a sleeping bag liner if you feel the cold.

What should we bring?

A small day bag (big bags are stored out of the way), a warm layer, a head torch, a hat, sunscreen, mosquito repellent, a towel, sandals, a power bank, and cash in Egyptian pounds for temple tickets.

Can we go all the way to Luxor?

Yes, with the 4-day cruise: the felucca takes you to Edfu, then a private minibus drives you to the temple of Horus and on to Luxor.

Can we see Abu Simbel?

Yes, as an option the day before the 4-day cruise: departure at 4 am by private minibus to the temple of Abu Simbel, back at your hotel in Aswan around 1:30 pm.

Can the programme be adapted?

Yes. The length of the trip, the departure date, the time of your arrival or your train, the pace and the stops are agreed with Tarek on WhatsApp before you leave.
